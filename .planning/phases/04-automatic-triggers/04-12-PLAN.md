  <files>src/Settings/SettingsModel.swift</files>
  <action>Declare `protocol TriggerControlling: Sendable` with `func update(_ settings: TriggerSettings) async`, `func currentSettings() async -> TriggerSettings` and `func authorizationNotice() async -> String?`, and add `extension TriggerCoordinator: TriggerControlling {}` — a retroactive conformance in this file, so 04-11's actor is not edited and SettingsModel is testable with a fake and no CoreLocation.
Add to `SettingsModel`: `private let triggers: (any TriggerControlling)?` as an init parameter WITHOUT a default (nil is legal and means the app came up with no coordinator, matching how the queue coordinator is already optional in the composition root), plus observable `private(set) var triggerSettings: TriggerSettings = .initial` and `private(set) var triggerNotice: String?`.
- `func loadTriggers() async` — reads `currentSettings()` and `authorizationNotice()` into those two properties. Called from the view's `.task`.
- `func setSignificantChange(_ on: Bool) async`, `setVisits(_:)`, `setGeofence(_:)` — each copies `triggerSettings`, flips only its own flag, assigns it locally so the switch moves immediately, then `await triggers?.update(newSettings)` and refreshes `triggerNotice` from `authorizationNotice()`. Refreshing the notice after the update is what makes a refused Always prompt produce its sentence without the user leaving the screen.
- `func setMinimumInterval(_ seconds: TimeInterval) async` — same shape, going through `TriggerSettings.setMinimumInterval` so the clamp applies even if a caller passes something smaller.
Persistence is the coordinator's job (it owns the store, 04-11), so nothing here writes UserDefaults and `save()` is untouched — the Save button stays about credentials.</action>
